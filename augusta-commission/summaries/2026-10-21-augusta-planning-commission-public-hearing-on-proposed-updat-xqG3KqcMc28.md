## TL;DR

* This was a special called Planning Commission public hearing on the draft rewrite of Augusta's comprehensive zoning ordinance, the first major update in more than 60 years (the current ordinance dates to 1963). Consultant White Smith Cousineau (WSC) presented. No vote was taken; staff recommended receiving the hearing as information, and a final draft is to return to the Planning Commission in December [00:14:04] [00:20:06] [02:48:51] [02:50:21].
* Major proposed changes: a form-based downtown district, two in-town neighborhood districts, new mixed-use districts, accessory dwelling units (ADUs) in all residential districts, duplexes to four-unit buildings in newer districts, buffer and parking updates, agricultural and estate residential options, and more administrative approvals [00:33:04] [00:35:20] [00:41:04] [00:47:21].
* About 25 public speakers took part. Most addressed short-term rentals (STRs), even though Planning Director Cameron opened by saying the STR section is an incomplete framework and the 14-day "Augusta rule" will not be eliminated [00:08:13] [00:09:07]. Others raised data centers, public notice and readable maps, ADU size caps, developer concerns about lot standards and density caps, a cat cafe use, and accessible housing.

### Consultant presentation (Justin Wallace, WSC)

* Justin Wallace is the project manager and lead drafter. Mark White, a firm partner, land use attorney, and planner, also attended. Partners are CoLab (Miami area) and Benchmark Planning (Charlotte area) [00:11:46] [00:13:41].
* A QR code links to the project website and the draft, which Wallace believed is dated October 14. A community survey is still open [00:12:21].
* Timeline: work began October 2024, the existing-ordinance review finished May 2025, and the first draft came out September 2025. The project is in its final phase [00:16:46] [00:17:09] [00:17:32].
* The subdivision, historic preservation, and tree ordinances are separate and are not being rewritten [00:16:17].
* Findings [00:21:20] [00:22:00] [00:23:08]:
  * 90% of existing R-2 lots and 85% of R-3 lots are smaller than the current minimum lot size.
  * A 2025 regional housing study (SRS Community Reuse Organization) expects more than 17,000 new homes in five years. Nearly half of households rent, and 6 in 10 people who work in Augusta commute from outside the county.
  * A review of 2021 to 2025 cases found recurring issues with compatibility, lot dimensions and setbacks, historic properties, buffers, site design, and unclear review procedures.
* Engagement: four open houses with 172 participants, stakeholder focus groups, and two online surveys with more than 800 responses. The first survey had 555 responses; 97% agreed zoning shapes Augusta's future, and East Augusta made up 5% of responses. The second survey, launched in August, has more than 280 responses [00:24:59] [00:25:33] [00:26:08] [00:27:10].
* A draft future land use map is new for Augusta. Current and proposed zoning maps were on boards in the lobby, with a conversion table on page 8 of the executive summary [00:29:38] [00:31:30] [00:31:42].
* District changes: rename 11 districts, consolidate 10, and create 7 new districts and overlays, including mixed-use districts, a downtown district, and a transit-oriented overlay for commercial corridors. The slide was introduced as "four types of changes," but only three were described [00:30:21] [00:30:57].
* Downtown: a form-based district with three subareas (downtown core, riverfront, cultural arts). It replaces the existing B-2 and industrial zoning downtown. Historic overlay and preservation requirements do not change [00:33:04] [00:33:53].
* In-town neighborhoods: two new districts, Middle Neighborhood and Neighborhood Residential, recalibrate lot sizes, widths, and setbacks. They allow ADUs, duplexes, triplexes, four-unit buildings, and small neighborhood businesses such as corner stores, framed as a response to food deserts [00:35:11] [00:35:35] [00:35:59].
* Mixed use: a mixed-use medical district around the medical employment center and a Neighborhood Center district modeled on places like Summerville [00:37:07] [00:37:40].
* Commercial corridors (Washington Road, Wrightsboro Road, Tobacco Road, Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard): standards for pedestrian and bicycle connections, landscaping, and parking; townhouses and live-work units allowed; screening buffers next to residential areas; and separation rules for uses such as vape shops and liquor stores [00:38:25] [00:39:57] [00:40:12].
* Housing: ADUs allowed in all residential districts, more duplexes to four-unit homes with design rules, and live-work and corner-store buildings with apartments above [00:41:04] [00:41:27] [00:41:59].
* Countywide standards: accessory structure setbacks aligned with building codes, open space for larger residential buildings, stronger buffer yards, flexible parking with electric vehicle and bicycle standards, and updates for state law [00:42:38] [00:43:05] [00:43:45].
* Rural: an updated agricultural district (urban agriculture, community gardens, beekeeping, farm stands, agritourism), a new estate residential district (transcribed "a state residential"), and an updated conservation development option [00:44:46] [00:45:26] [00:45:46].
* Process: clearer review procedures, fewer special exceptions in favor of objective standards, and more administrative approvals, including staff adjustments to some dimensional standards such as setbacks [00:46:40] [00:46:59] [00:47:21].
