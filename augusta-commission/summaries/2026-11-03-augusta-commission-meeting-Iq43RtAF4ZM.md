## TL;DR

* The consent agenda (items 1 to 22, plus 23 and 25) passed, as the clerk put it, "unanimous with Mr. Garrett absent." The list includes an on-premise liquor, beer, wine, and dance license for Chevy's Nightclub at 3328 Washington Road.
* A $78,695 sole-source purchase of a 30-foot artificial Christmas tree package for the Augusta Common passed over Commissioner Wimberly's skepticism. Staff expect it to replace a $13,000 to $15,000 live tree each year.
* The Greater Augusta Arts Council asked for approval to buy three sculptures for $15,200, to accept two donated works, and to adopt an updated public art policy. Its city MOU expires this month. The transcript records no vote on these requests.
* Short-term rental rules in the draft comprehensive zoning ordinance update are still under consideration. An STR operator spoke against restrictions. The mayor said personally that investors are running "a hotel" inside single-family neighborhoods.
* Right-of-way cleanup in District 2 is under way. A grounds management department work session was set for November 12 (transcribed separately).

## Discussion by Agenda Item

### Opening and recognitions

* Reverend Gary Carr of First Baptist Church of Keysville served as chaplain of the day [00:08:50].
* HR (Shadonis DePalmer for Director Anita Rooker) recognized October service anniversaries: 25 employees totaling 370 years [00:14:11]. Honorees with 25 or more years: Alinda Harrison (Tax Commissioner, 25), Chris Mullen (IT, 30), William Hornsby (Sheriff, 30), Dana Toatka (Marshal, 35), and George Clemons (Recreation and Parks, 40) [00:14:39].

### State Veterans Cemetery (personal privilege, Commissioner Clark)

* The groundbreaking is Friday, November 13, at 10:30 a.m. on 200-plus acres behind Gracewood. Clark called it the largest veterans cemetery in Georgia, with no residency requirement [00:18:14].
* The effort dates to 1999 under former Mayor Bob Young. It stalled after 9/11 because the first site was on Fort Gordon [00:18:44].
* The mayor noted the Veterans Day service on November 11 at 11 a.m. at the All Wars Memorial [00:20:52].

### Greater Augusta Arts Council (delegation, Heather Dunaway)

* Dunaway presented a 2026 progress report against the 2017 Public Art Master Plan: sculpture trail expansion, murals, accessible installations, and two seasonal tours (Promenade and Paint, Augusta Sculpture Trail guided tour) [00:31:41].
* Purchase request: Pale Flame (Joe Scher, lower Riverwalk), Crescendo (Sam Spitzka, Augusta Common), and Hearts at Play (Melinda Hoffman, voted number one by the public). The total is $15,200 of a $20,000 allocation, with the remainder going to installation [00:32:45]. All three are already installed [00:35:14].
* Donations to accept: Nitrogenous (funded by a Destination Augusta grant) and Princess Augusta (from the Woman's Club of Augusta, for the Riverwalk near the book bench). The trail would grow from 18 to 23 sculptures [00:33:19].
* Policy update: the advisory panel grows from 7 to 8 members, the chair votes only to break ties, and the goal is representation from every district [00:36:02].
* The MOU naming the Arts Council as the city's public art agency expires this month. City staff and the Council are drafting a new one, and it will come back to the commission for approval (Attorney Plunkett, answering Commissioner Pulliam) [00:37:56].

### Short-term rentals (delegation, Joanna Nabholz)

* Nabholz splits time between Seattle and Augusta, owns several STR properties, and plans to relocate her family [00:22:52].
* Her arguments: STRs serve families, medical-treatment guests (one guest stays monthly for about 10 days), weddings, and Peach Jam, not just the Masters. Operators screen guests and respond quickly. STRs employ cleaners, electricians, and plumbers and send guests to local businesses [00:23:52].
* She asked for clear rules and said operators want to be treated as respectful businesses [00:25:41].
* Speaking personally, the mayor said investors buying single-family homes to run de facto hotels concerns neighborhoods [00:27:40].
* Another STR delegation (Rose Evans, on STR provisions in the draft comprehensive zoning ordinance update) was called but absent [00:29:27].

### Artificial Christmas tree (addendum)

* The $78,695 sole source to Downtown Decoration Inc. buys a 30-foot tree package with star, lights, and ornaments. It carries a 5-year warranty and an expected 10-year life, ships free, and will be displayed in the Augusta Common [00:45:04].
* Director Williams (Recreation and Parks) said recent live trees looked like "Charlie Brown" trees and cost $13,000 to $15,000 a year. She said the purchase is covered within about five years and is funded from savings because the Common hosted fewer events [00:45:35].
* Commissioner Wimberly asked whether the city simply picked trees too late, and said he was "not sold" on an artificial tree [00:47:17].
* The tree lighting is December 5 [00:45:35].
* Commissioner Clark asked about decorating the rest of downtown during construction. Recreation and Parks covers only the Common, and Engineering and Central Services handle the rest. Clark will raise it as a separate agenda item [00:49:52].

### Item 24: Right-of-way and vacant lot maintenance (Commissioner Pulliam)

* Administrator Allen reported cleanup in District 2 after Pulliam's photos the week before. Crews cleared a stop sign that brush had hidden at 8th Street and Grand Boulevard [00:52:56].
* Crews from Dr. Malik's team and the Warden's team did the work. Two crews remain assigned to District 2, including Turpin Hill [00:53:43].
* Vacant lots are handled separately from right-of-way and stay under Environmental Services. Staff are inventorying lots and will report on any extra funding needed [00:53:22].
* Pulliam added Winter Street (near Virginia Avenue and Norfolk, off Mike Padgett) and Nellieville. She said one site had not been touched since 2024 and called for a lasting plan rather than "a band-aid" [00:56:25].
* Pulliam recalled that part of the garbage collection fee was earmarked for vacant lots [00:56:53].
* A grounds management department work session was set for November 12, 2:30 to 4:30 p.m., in the Beasley Room, covering org charts and budget. The public is invited [00:55:04].
