## TL;DR

* This was a workshop with no motions or votes. Deputy Administrator Charles Jackson presented the transition plan for a new Grounds Management Department, targeted to launch January 1, 2027. It combines grounds work now split across Engineering, Central Services, and Recreation and Parks under a new director, Mr. Garvin, who started that Monday [00:02:04] [00:06:56].
* The department's draft budget is $7.4 million, with contracts at nearly 70% and in-house personnel at about 30%. The stated goal is to minimize fiscal impact using existing resources [00:07:17] [00:07:58].
* The plan leans on RCCI inmate crews, which Jackson called the "secret sauce." Civilian crew leaders, each limited to three inmates, would form "floating crews" for residential hot spots. Districts 1 and 2 generate nearly 40% of service requests [00:21:11] [00:29:44] [00:53:07].
* Commissioners said consolidation improves accountability but does not raise cut frequency. Ponds are cut once a year on current schedules. They asked staff to return options and costs for more cuts, a stormwater breakdown, and equipment replacement estimates, with a request to have them before the holiday break [00:45:08] [01:37:18] [01:49:33].
* Vacant lots stay outside the new department, and staff said no plan exists within it. A commissioner asked administration to start a vacant lot plan, which echoes Commissioner Pulliam's November 3 call for a lasting fix [00:43:06] [01:38:36].

### Timeline, budget, and transition status

* Completed so far: budget, personnel, and equipment data gathered from involved departments; job descriptions discussed with HR; and a draft budget that Ms. Allen will discuss "next week" [00:05:28].
* Higgins was appointed transition lead. Jackson credited him with 90% of the research, and later 95% [00:05:56] [00:37:08].

### Why one department

* Three departments provide maintenance today under different operating models [00:08:41].
* Jackson's case for consolidation: shift personnel between recreation sites, right-of-way, ditches, and ponds; cross-train staff; adopt one SOP for equipment, inspection, documentation, and training; and have one director to hold accountable [00:08:53] [00:09:33] [00:10:09].

### Recommendations

* Jackson asked the public and commissioners to weigh in on priorities: safety and line-of-sight areas (such as stop signs hidden by vegetation), residential areas in Districts 1 and 2, commercial corridors, and GDOT corridors [00:27:46].

* RCCI: civilian crew leaders would be tied to inmate crews to form floating crews aimed at hot spots, along with equipment, PPE, and daily work records. Jackson reported consensus with Warden McMillan [00:29:44] [00:30:45].
