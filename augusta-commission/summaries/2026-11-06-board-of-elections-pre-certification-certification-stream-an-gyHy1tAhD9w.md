## TL;DR

* Under the State Election Board pre-certification rule, staff reported five precincts where ballots scanned and voter credit differed by one. The net difference was negative three ballots versus credit for voting. Precinct 201 (581 ballots, 580 credits) is unexplained so far. No duplicate voters were found. The board voted that it had complied with the rule.
* There were 96 provisional ballots: the board counted 72 and rejected 24. With 14 timely military absentee ballots added, the total rose from 67,004 to 67,090 ballots, or 50.02% of 134,116 registered voters. The vote on Group C (20 ballots rejected) was called, but its outcome is not audible.
* Results were read into the record, and the board voted to certify the November 3, 2026 general and special election. As a precaution, board members also signed a separate certification for the Board of Education special election.

A second advance voting site at Warren Road was approved for November 23 to 25, conditional on a written Board of Education request and an agreement to pay costs.
* The risk-limiting audit starts November 13 at 9 a.m., after the state dice roll on November 12. The 2027 elections budget request is $1.1 million, down $679,000, because no elections are scheduled. The board asked staff and legal counsel to report on options for the pre-certification rule.

## Discussion by Agenda Item

### Pre-certification review (State Election Board rule)

* The rule requires the board to meet by 3 p.m. on the Friday after the election and review ballots cast by vote method for each precinct [00:00:05]. The packet categories are election day, AIP (absentee in person), ABM (absentee by mail), SOVC (statement of votes cast, meaning ballots counted election night), and Accepted (voters from the numbered list and poll pad) [00:00:45].
* Precinct 201: the statement of votes cast shows 581 ballots, while credit for voting shows 580. The poll pad, scanner, and BMD paperwork all show 581, and staff are still investigating [00:01:26].
* Precinct 202: 830 ballots were counted and 831 voters received credit. The poll manager noted that a voter may have walked out without scanning a ballot [00:02:15].
* Precinct 403: 328 ballots were scanned and 329 voters received credit [00:02:56]. Precinct 504: on one day of absentee in-person voting, the poll pads showed one more voter than ballots scanned [00:03:18]. Precinct 804H: 656 voters received credit and 655 ballots were scanned [00:03:41].
* Net result: negative three ballots versus credit for voting [00:03:51].
* Duplicate check: staff demonstrated an Excel COUNTIF filter on the voter list instead of a manual scan of 67,010 voters. The only duplicate found was staff's own test entry [00:05:24], [00:10:50]. Staff said no errors had been discovered [00:11:49].
* On precinct 201, Director Doss said state law allows 30 days to reconcile discrepancies, but this rule compresses that to about two and a half days [00:12:24]. The board's voter list from the state registration system showed 580 with credit, possibly because of a voter status change or transfer [00:13:55]. Other reports, such as a poll pad report, had not been reviewed yet, and the cause "may not ever unveil itself." The 30-day report would explain it as fully as possible [00:14:23].
* For the four precincts at negative one (202, 403, 504, 804H), the director agreed the most likely explanation is that a voter checked in and left with the ballot [00:15:24].
* The board voted that it had complied with the State Election Board rule and prepared for certification [00:16:00].

### Risk-limiting audit (old business)

* Races subject to audit under "SB3EX" include U.S. Senate, U.S. House District 12, governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, attorney general, state school superintendent, and the commissioners of insurance, agriculture, and labor, plus public service commissioner [00:31:24].
* The Secretary of State's office estimated 12 to 14 teams, while staff had planned for 10 [00:32:00]. The dice roll is Thursday, November 12, at 10 a.m., and batch assignments arrive by noon. The local audit starts Friday, November 13, at 9 a.m. at the warehouse [00:37:03], [00:40:35].
* Advance voting used 16 scanners instead of the usual 8, and they were swapped at about 3,000 ballots to keep audit batches small. Sites had between 9,000 and 12,000 ballots each [00:32:47].
* The audit is open to the public. Observers are expected from (the director believes) the Carter Center and from South Carolina's Secretary of State's office [00:34:09].
* Two-person teams hand count ballots, and the totals are compared with the machine count. Batch selection depends on margins, so not every county audits every race [00:35:01]. The director said the number of audited offices rose from one or two to 12 or 14 after a special session [00:37:39].
* Richmond County had 100% accuracy in audits until May, when results were off by about three to five ballots. The director attributed this to his own administrative error in auditing two races at once. Teams will return to the sort-and-call method, one race at a time [00:39:03].
