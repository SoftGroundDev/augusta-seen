## TL;DR

* No votes were taken. Administrator Allen presented uses for the hotel-motel tax and answered eight budget concerns from Commissioner Wimberly. Wimberly said he still has "red flags" he will raise later.
* Augusta's 12.5% share of the hotel-motel tax (raised from 6% to 8% on October 1, 2025) is budgeted at about $1.54 million for 2027. Staff recommend it first repay Augusta Utilities $2.5 million for Patch golf course water and sewer work, then fund a new $250,000 festivals and special events grant program, with the rest held as contingency.
* The 2027 general and law enforcement fund budgets use no one-time money, ending reliance on ARPA. They include a fully funded 3% COLA and the recommended two mills; each half mill is a little over $4 million, or about $20 a year on a $100,000 home.
* Urban homeowners would pay modestly more (mostly urban services millage for transit and fire); suburban homeowners only a few dollars a year. Water and sewer rises by less than $5 a month for the average household.
* The 49 frozen sheriff's positions stay unfunded (about $4 million a year to restore). HCD's 64 rental homes (57 occupied), managed by Right Choice Property Management at $95 per unit a month, drew questions about word-of-mouth tenant placement, and commissioners asked for a written applicant process.

### Opening and framing

### Hotel-motel excise tax

* The 2025 General Assembly raised the tax from 6% to 8%, effective October 1, 2025 [00:05:37]. Destination Augusta and the Coliseum Authority each receive 43.75%, and Augusta receives 12.5% [00:06:00].
* Augusta's share was about $337,000 in 2025 (the new rate applied only a few months), is projected at about $1.5 million for 2026, and is budgeted at about $1.54 million for 2027 [00:06:28].
* First recommended use: reimburse Augusta Utilities $2.5 million, the city's committed share of water and sewer infrastructure for the Patch project at the Augusta Municipal Golf Course [00:07:00]. The repayment schedule was not stated.
* Second, new for 2027: a festivals and special events grants program with a $250,000 pot for events that create a meaningful tourism draw. Destination Augusta suggested the concept [00:07:26] [00:07:58].
* Staff cited Columbia and Charlotte as regional models and the tourism grant program Destination Augusta previously managed [00:08:12]. Allen said related funding was cut from the 2026 general fund, as the Greater Augusta Arts Council reminded the body the prior Tuesday [00:08:42].
* Broad Street construction nearing completion and SPLOST 9 facility improvements should add venues [00:09:04].
* Plan: form an evaluation committee with community stakeholders such as Destination Augusta, finalize criteria, then advertise, evaluate applications, and bring recommended awards to the commission. Staff would return in Q1 2027 for full program approval [00:09:25] [00:09:39].
* Remaining 2027 funds are unprogrammed and budgeted as contingency, possibly to supplement major recreational capital projects [00:10:07].
* Commissioner Pulliam asked about eligible uses. Staff said anything that promotes tourism, per the resolution, rather than a specific law [00:10:46] [00:11:24].
* Commissioner Scott asked who administers the fund. It stays in the city's finance budget and is not handed to another organization [00:11:45] [00:12:11].

### Concerns 1 and 6: Structural balance and rising costs

* Allen listed Wimberly's eight concerns on one slide. The sheriff's frozen positions and grounds management will get their own work sessions [00:12:46] [00:13:13].
* The budget is structurally balanced. It ends reliance on one-time money, mostly ARPA, and matches rising costs with recurring revenue [00:13:42].
* One-time support: ARPA in 2024 and 2025, then the last ARPA dollars plus a one-time transfer of hiring-freeze savings in 2026. It drops to zero in 2027, and every general and law enforcement fund revenue source will recur [00:14:28] [00:15:00].
* Health care is the largest cost driver. Employee premium increases, prescription cost containment, and plan changes began in 2026 and continue in 2027 with the health care consultants [00:15:40] [00:16:03].
* The 3% COLA is fully funded for 2027 and future years [00:16:21]. Software contract cost growth is called out for review in an upcoming efficiency study of enterprise systems [00:16:29].
* The budget adds no new services or personnel [00:16:47].
* Commissioner Clark asked whether landfill, trash, and street light fees are covered. Allen said they are in the overall proposal [00:17:12].
* Outlook: a rough look ahead, not a formal forecast, assumes another 3% COLA in each of the next two years and shows "modest gaps" [00:17:49] [00:18:06].
* Sales tax is the biggest uncertainty. Collections have slowed, and the 2027 budget is set at the level expected for 2026 [00:18:18] [00:19:06] [00:19:21].

### Concern 2: Millage scenarios

* Wimberly asked for scenarios from zero to the recommended two mills. Each half mill is a little over $4 million across the two funds and about $20 a year on a $100,000 home [00:19:45].
* Without the recommended millage, the gap could only be closed with deep service cuts. With the new FLOST rollback, the county rate ends up slightly lower than this year even with two mills [00:20:08].
* Any lower scenario would require substantial departmental cuts on top of this year's 5% reductions [00:20:23].
