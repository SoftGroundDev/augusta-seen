### Item A: COA-26-33, 2627 Raymond Avenue, window replacement (Summerville)

* Todd Kennedy (Planning and Development) presented the case: a 3,500 square foot home from 1910 on about 0.2 acres, zoned one-family residential. The owner wants to replace 29 wood windows with vinyl [00:07:08].
* The applicant's quotes were just over $38,000 for vinyl, a little over $114,000 for wood, and just under $122,000 for vinyl-clad wood [00:08:37].
* Historic Augusta recommended vinyl-clad or aluminum-clad wood windows and asked why replacement was needed, since the guidelines treat replacement as a last resort [00:09:29]. Staff recommended denial and suggested the applicant repair where feasible and return with a proposal consistent with the guidelines [00:10:03].
* Owner Lawless Dyer said a storm destroyed most of the existing vinyl storm windows and he could not find replacements [00:12:47]. He pointed to earlier approved work (Hardie board siding over aluminum and a slate roof replacing an asbestos one) [00:13:13] and said 40% of houses on Raymond have vinyl windows [00:13:52]. He asked for an exception [00:15:02] and said he would not spend $100,000 on windows [00:26:15].
* Neighbor Shane Claffey, a 42-year Summerville resident and one of the authors of the 1997 guidelines, opposed the request. She said drafty windows come with a historic district [00:16:23] and noted that about two years earlier a similar request for 30-some vinyl windows on Henry Street was denied [00:17:24].
* Commissioner John questioned the price gap based on his experience restoring eight historic houses [00:19:08] [00:27:06]. He said vinyl devalues the house and suggested interior storm windows, which Historic Augusta has used [00:19:41]. He said other vinyl windows in the area were likely installed illegally or before designation [00:25:06].
* Another commissioner confirmed most of the windows are not operable [00:24:11]. A third asked about interior storm windows, and Dyer said he would look at them [00:24:40].
* A motion phrased as "reject" was restated by the chair as accepting the staff recommendation to deny. It carried 6-0 [00:27:55].

### Item B: COA-26-34, 11 Ellis Street, replacement sign (Old Town)

* The building is a non-contributing 1990 storefront on just under half an acre at Ellis and East Boundary. A new tenant has taken over [00:29:18] [00:31:11].
* Historic Augusta said the sign appears to meet the Old Town guidelines. Staff confirmed it meets the sign code [00:30:23] [00:31:31].
* The four conditions: conform to the 3 ft by 30 ft elevation with a 5 ft circular logo; avoid damaging facade elements and anchor in mortar joints; submit illumination, electrical, and attachment details for staff review; and obtain all other permits [00:32:56].
* Russell Trapp (Moog Signs, 333 East Boundary) said the existing Lucy's Laundry letters are being moved from another building [00:34:10]. The motion passed 6-0 [00:35:26].

### Item C: COA-26-35, 2609 Bellevue, roof material change (Summerville)

* This is a 1983 townhome zoned R-3B. The owner, Martha Baxter, wants to change the rear roof from asphalt shingle to standing seam metal [00:36:05].
* Historic Augusta said the work does not appear visible from the street but recommended a narrower 12 to 16 inch seam instead of 18 inches [00:36:54]. Staff's five conditions specify a 17-inch pan limited to the rear roof, staff review of color, seam profile, and trim, no change to roof form, maintained flashing and drainage, and review of any deviations [00:38:21].
* John Barnhart (JMR Builders) said the area is a separate single-slope shed roof across a courtyard, not half of a pitched roof [00:39:43]. He confirmed the pan is 17 inches, not 18, and said a narrower seam was possible [00:41:05] [00:41:27].
* The reason for the change is lightning damage at the parapet wall and insulation installed incorrectly, causing moisture problems [00:41:46]. The motion to approve with staff recommendations passed 6-0 [00:42:47].

### Item F: COA-26-38, 1224 Johns Road, new wood fence (Summerville)

* The 4 ft treated wood fence ties into an existing fence, runs about 45 ft southwesterly and about 90 ft along the Queensway side, then about 20 ft diagonally to the house, with a gate [00:44:00].
* Historic Augusta noted that the photos appeared to show a 6 ft fence. Staff confirmed with the applicant that it will be 4 ft under the fence ordinance [00:45:08]. Staff recommended approval on two conditions: wood, treated as presented, and limited to 4 ft, with any deviation requiring HPC review or a BZA variance [00:47:31].
* Patrick May (Heritage Augusta, 2228 Queensway) said the original 6 ft plan was cut to 4 ft because it is a corner lot. A variance could not be heard until January because of the November and December meeting schedule [00:48:13].
* Owner Shane Claffey said she was burglarized through the unseen rear of the corner lot [00:49:46]. She owns about three-quarters of a 371 ft block [00:50:23] and wants a 6 ft middle section, painted dark green and screened with landscaping around a 130-year-old magnolia [00:51:04] [00:57:09]. She said she is willing to go to zoning [00:54:01].
* Kennedy said Queensway is a public street, so a street-side fence is treated like a front yard: 4 ft within about 30 ft of the property line, and 6 ft beyond that [00:58:32]. The existing 6 ft fence complies because it is on an interior side [01:02:02].
* Commissioner John noted that height is outside the HPC's purview and a 6 ft fence would still need a BZA variance [01:02:25]. His motion to approve at 4 ft, up to no more than 6 ft pending zoning approval, passed 6-0 [01:06:10].
