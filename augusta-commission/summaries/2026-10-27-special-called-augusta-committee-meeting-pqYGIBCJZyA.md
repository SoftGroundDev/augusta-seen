### ISM additional fees, $379,795.19 (Public Services item 5)

* Director Williams (Recreation and Parks) said the fees cover out-of-scope work over about four and a half years: design services, assessments, special inspections and testing, and appraisals done by ISM subcontractors since 2022 [03:19:02].
* Commissioner Garrett, not a committee member, asked why the work was not billed as it accrued and said he did not want to set a new precedent [03:20:22]. Another commissioner said they had not seen this in eight years on the body [03:20:58].
* The ISM representative said ISM fronted costs, starting with concrete and soil testing at Henry Brigham, until on-call engineering firms were in place, and charged no markup [03:21:21]. Subcontractors have already been paid [03:22:33]. He described it as a "zero percent interest loan" to the city [03:24:28].
* Williams said the cost is covered in her budget and finance has agreed [03:23:09], from what was transcribed as "recapture of the opera funds" [03:23:25].
* The mayor pro tem listed the projects in the bill: Big Oak, Diamond Lakes sidewalks, Henry Brigham, Jamestown Community Center, Fleming Tennis Center, Newman Tennis Center, and park utilization [03:25:02]. ISM said its contract covers project management, not preliminary and final design, which it funded to push Big Oak forward [03:25:39].
* Project status from ISM: all but four projects should be under construction or bid by year end [03:27:28]. Henry Brigham is done, Jamestown should finish in two to three weeks, and plans for Big Oak, Boykin Road, Diamond Lakes, Dyess Park, Fleming, May Park, and Newman Tennis Center are 100 percent complete [03:33:40]. The contract is five years under SPLOST 8 [03:28:05]. Permitting has improved working with Ms. Cameron [03:32:55].
* Commissioner Johnson asked about Dyess Park. ISM said it is in permitting and should be under construction within two to three months [03:37:16].
* Commissioner Scott moved approval [03:38:03].

### Right-of-way and vacant lot scheduling (Engineering item 10, Commissioner Pulliam)

* Pulliam showed her own video of overgrowth from Nellieville to Sullivan Road, including a blind stop-sign intersection [03:56:34], 15th Avenue into Turpin Hill [03:57:08], 8th Avenue near Old Savannah Road [03:58:16], and Winter Street, Nixon Road, and Norfolk Street [03:59:24]. She said it has looked this way for all four years she has been in office [03:58:27].
* She asked for maintenance schedules that rotate through areas even without new funding [03:59:51], noted some areas are pitch black at night [04:02:06], and asked for a citywide review of neglected areas that raise safety concerns [04:03:21].
* Dr. Malik said the work is reactive and resource-limited [04:04:18]. Many cities make owners maintain up to the back of the curb, but he could not find that in Augusta's code [04:12:40]. District 2 has 186 centerline miles of road [04:05:26].
* Crews: four inmate crews from the warden plus two civilian work details. One crew handles commissioner requests and another picks up cut material [04:05:57]. In a 2024 workshop he had asked for 14 crews [04:07:04].
* Engineering gets more than 6,000 requests a year, which he estimated at 70 to 80 percent of city requests [04:14:27]. His July request was $8.2 million: $3.2 million roadway, $3.5 million for ditches, $1 million for vacant lots, and $500 (likely $500,000) for illegal dumping [04:15:14].
* Commissioner Johnson said the Sullivan and Dalwood Terrace areas have looked like this his whole life [04:12:13]. He asked Malik to draft code language with legal making owners responsible [04:13:03] and to estimate costs for priority areas. Malik agreed to price Turpin Hill and the Nixon Road area [04:16:25].
* Cleanup costs on private property are recovered as a lien on the property tax bill; a property can be auctioned if the lien exceeds its value, and many go to the land bank [04:17:39].
* Commissioner Rice asked whether owners are responsible for part of the overgrowth; Malik said it is a combination [04:10:31]. Rice confirmed the new department will take over this work [04:11:27].
* Chair Clark called it a countywide, multi-tiered issue of prison crews, internal teams, and contracts, and said the new division is meant to centralize it [04:18:39].
* Mayor Pro Tem Guilfoyle suggested rotating one warden crew through the area weekly and using the dormant season to get ahead [04:21:07]. Malik said he has two months before the work transitions [04:22:13].
* Commissioner Wimberly said the root problem is funding: "no new taxes is what got us here" [04:24:41].
* Pulliam asked staff to at least mow the street edges for sight lines, since there are no streetlights [04:26:01]. Malik said the rule of thumb for owner responsibility is the back of the curb or sidewalk [04:28:07], and the stop sign and power pole likely mark the edge of the right-of-way [04:28:57]. Pulliam will send the administrator locations with missing street signs [04:29:23].
* The administrator said Districts 1 and 2 have the most overgrowth and vacant lot calls [04:30:00]. Mr. Jackson and the transition lead have gathered data for a plan, including a way for residents to see owner versus city responsibility [04:30:23], to be presented at a public work session the week of November 9 [04:30:52]. Meanwhile she will meet with Malik and Jackson to put crews on the area [04:31:10].
* Clark noted a vine-covered light pole and a broken power pole not shown in the video [04:31:42]. The item was forwarded to the commission, with the administrator to report back and to include the warden [04:33:00].
